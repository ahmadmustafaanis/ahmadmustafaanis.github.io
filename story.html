Chapter 01 · High school

My story did not begin with a perfect plan.

It began with Dota 2, slipping grades, and a university I had not dreamed of attending.

IIUI · 2018

Then I fell in love with computer science.

I entered a lower-ranked CS program at IIUI. I wanted to make games—although I never actually made one. What stayed was the joy of building things with code.

Then I watched an OpenAI bot play Dota. The same game that had hurt my grades showed me what artificial intelligence could become.

“I have to go into AI.”

No roadmap. Start anyway.

When the resources were not around me, I went looking for them.

There was no established AI path at my university. So I built my own curriculum, one lecture and one assignment at a time.

Second year. Third year.

Two summers passed without an internship.

I kept trying. Near the end of the second summer, a mutual connection introduced me to a tiny three-person AI services startup.

It was a small door. It opened into real work, difficult problems, and the kind of learning no course could reproduce.

Persistence, repeated

I applied six or seven times.

Red Buffer was where Pakistan’s only two Google Developer Experts in AI worked. I wanted to learn from them, so I kept applying.

In my final semester, they hired me as a junior machine learning engineer. I skipped my remaining classes and went all-in on the full-time job.

A name in an NVIDIA talk

One reply changed the questions I wanted to ask.

After graduation, I was working on pose estimation at Red Buffer. In an NVIDIA GTC talk, the speaker kept citing “Iqbal et al.” Iqbal is a common Pakistani name, so I looked it up.

It was researcher Umar Iqbal. I sent him a message. He replied in detail.

That conversation pulled research within reach. It was also the first time I heard of CVPR.

Cohere Labs · 2022

I joined a small Discord. Then I kept showing up.

The Cohere Labs community had around 400 members. I attended, asked questions, and—with encouragement from Robert Joseph—gave my first research-community talk on OpenAI’s CLIP.

When applications opened for community leads, I applied without any leadership experience. I became the first regional lead for Asia.

Showing up became contributing. Contributing became leading.

Build what you once needed

Community gave me access. I learned to create access for others.

I taught myself cold outreach and hosted researchers from leading universities across Asia. The community also connected me to the Data Provenance Initiative, Aya, and collaborators for my own research.

Four years of graduate-school rejections

The answer finally changed.

While applying, I kept working—two years at Red Buffer, then two and a half years at Silicon Valley startup Roll.ai. I published with collaborators at NeurIPS, ICLR, and a CVPR workshop.

Then Carnegie Mellon said yes. Today, I research self-supervised learning and computer vision at CMU’s TarrLab.

The story is still being written.

Your starting point is not your ceiling.

I did not begin at a famous university, with perfect grades, a research network, or a clear path. I began by being curious—and then returning to that curiosity after every no.

Show up. Ask. Build. Apply again.
